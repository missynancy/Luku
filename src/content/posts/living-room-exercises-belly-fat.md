## Start with a simple routine

A simple living room workout you can do to target belly fat — no gym, no equipment, just consistency. You do not need fancy equipment to move your body and improve your fitness. These five exercises work your core, raise your heart rate, and can all be done on a mat, rug, or bare floor.

### The 5 exercises

 
## 1. Squats
 
**Why it works:** Squats work your quads, hamstrings, and glutes, and engage your core the entire time. It's one of the most efficient full-lower-body movements there is.
 
**How to do it:**
- Stand with feet shoulder-width apart
- Lower your hips back and down like you're sitting into a chair, keeping your chest up and knees tracking over your toes
- Go as low as feels comfortable (aim for thighs parallel to the floor)
- Push through your heels to stand back up
**Reps:** 3 sets of 15
 
**Progression tip:** Once 15 feels easy, slow down the lowering phase (count to 4 on the way down) to increase the challenge without adding weight.
 
## 2. Push-Ups
 
**Why it works:** A true full-upper-body movement — chest, shoulders, triceps, and core all fire at once.
 
**How to do it:**
- Start in a plank position, hands slightly wider than shoulder-width
- If you're just starting out, drop to your knees to reduce the load
- Lower your chest toward the floor with control, then push back up
- Keep your body in a straight line throughout — don't let your hips sag
**Reps:** 3 sets of 10 (knees down is completely fine — full form on knees beats sloppy form on toes)
 
**Progression tip:** Once knee push-ups feel easy, try just 2–3 reps on your toes at the end of each set, and build up from there.
 
## 3. Plank Holds
 
**Why it works:** This is the most underrated move for belly fat because it trains your entire core — including the deep stabilizing muscles that flatten your midsection over time, not just the visible "six-pack" muscles.
 
**How to do it:**
- Rest on your forearms and toes, elbows directly under your shoulders
- Keep your body in a straight line from head to heels
- Squeeze your glutes and pull your belly button in toward your spine
- Breathe steadily — don't hold your breath
**Reps:** 3 sets of 30 seconds
 
**Progression tip:** Add 5–10 seconds each week. If 30 seconds is genuinely hard right now, start at 15–20 and build up — consistency matters more than hitting a number on day one.
 
## 4. Glute Bridges
 
**Why it works:** If you sit at a desk most of the day (very common for remote workers), your glutes tend to switch off and your lower back overcompensates. This move directly counters that.
 
**How to do it:**
- Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat on the floor hip-width apart
- Push through your heels to lift your hips toward the ceiling, squeezing your glutes at the top
- Lower back down with control
**Reps:** 3 sets of 15
 
**Progression tip:** At the top of the movement, hold for 2 seconds before lowering — this extra time under tension makes the same 15 reps noticeably harder.
 
## 5. Mountain Climbers
 
**Why it works:** This is your cardio for the day. It raises your heart rate quickly while still working your core and shoulders, so you get a fat-burning effect without needing to run anywhere.
 
**How to do it:**
- Start in a plank position on your hands
- Drive one knee toward your chest, then quickly switch legs, as if you're running in place horizontally
- Keep your hips low and steady — don't let them bounce up and down
**Reps:** 3 sets of 20 (count each leg as one rep, so 20 total switches)
 
**Progression tip:** Increase your speed once your form is solid, rather than adding more reps — a faster, controlled pace raises the cardio benefit significantly.
 
## Putting It All Together
 
Do all five exercises back-to-back as one circuit, resting 30–60 seconds between exercises. Repeat the full circuit 3 times. That's your complete session — roughly 15–20 minutes, zero equipment, zero gym fees.
 
**A few honest notes:**
- If you're brand new to exercise, start with 2 sets instead of 3, and build up over the first couple of weeks
- Consistency beats intensity — three 15-minute sessions a week will outperform one brutal hour-long session followed by two weeks off
- If any movement causes sharp pain (not just muscle fatigue), stop and check with a doctor before continuing
