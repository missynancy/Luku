When building an affordable, high-protein Kenyan diet, two plant-based staples dominate the local market: **Ndengu (mung beans)** and **Kamande (brown lentils)**. Both are cheap, widely available at Naivas, Carrefour, and open-air markets, and packed with plant protein.

However, if your primary goal is **weight loss, appetite control, or building lean muscle**, which one should you choose?

This guide breaks down the exact nutritional differences, calorie density, protein content, and cooking benefits of **Ndengu vs Kamande** to help you make the smartest choice for your fitness goals.

## Nutritional Comparison: Ndengu vs Kamande (Per 100g Dry Weight)

| Nutritional Metric | Ndengu (Mung Beans) | Kamande (Brown Lentils) | Winner |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Protein (g)** | **24g** | **25g** | **Tie** (Both excellent) |
| **Fiber (g)** | **16g** | **8g - 11g** | **Ndengu** (Keeps you full longer) |
| **Calories (kcal)** | **347 kcal** | **350 kcal** | **Tie** |
| **Glycemic Index (GI)** | **25 (Very Low)** | **32 (Low)** | **Ndengu** (Slower blood sugar rise) |
| **Cooking Time** | ~45-60 mins (Needs soaking) | ~20-30 mins (No soaking needed) | **Kamande** (Much faster) |
| **Gas / Bloating Risk** | Moderate | Low | **Kamande** (Easier to digest) |
| **Avg. Cost per 1kg (KES)** | ~KES 180 - 220 | ~KES 200 - 250 | **Ndengu** (Slightly cheaper) |

---

## 1. Protein Content & Muscle Recovery
Both legumes are absolute powerhouses for plant protein. A 100g serving of dry Ndengu delivers **24g of protein**, while dry Kamande provides **25g of protein**. 

When paired with a complex carbohydrate source like brown rice or sweet potatoes, both foods provide a complete amino acid profile necessary for muscle repair and recovery after workouts.

---

## 2. Fiber & Weight Loss (Satiety)
If your main target is losing belly fat, **Ndengu takes a slight edge due to its higher dietary fiber content**.

- **Ndengu contains 16g of fiber per 100g**, which slows down digestion significantly. Eating a bowl of stewed ndengu for lunch keeps your stomach full well into the evening, preventing cravings for afternoon mandazi or chips.
- **Kamande contains ~10g of fiber per 100g**, which is still high compared to white rice or ugali, but digests slightly faster than ndengu.

---

## 3. Digestion and Bloating
One common complaint with legumes in Kenya is stomach gas and bloating.

- **Kamande is significantly easier on the stomach**. Brown lentils have thinner skins and lower levels of complex oligosaccharides, meaning they cause less bloating and flatulence.
- **Ndengu can cause mild gas** if not prepared correctly. To minimize bloating, always soak dry Ndengu in warm water for at least 4 to 6 hours (or overnight) and discard the soaking water before boiling.

---

## 4. Preparation & Convenience
For busy professionals and remote workers who want quick weeknight meal prep:

- **Kamande cooks in just 20 to 30 minutes** without needing any advance soaking. This makes Kamande the clear winner for quick dinner prep.
- **Ndengu requires 45 to 60 minutes of boiling** unless soaked beforehand or pressure cooked.

---

## The Verdict: Which One Should You Buy?

- **Choose Ndengu if:** Your main goal is **maximum weight loss, appetite control, and budget savings**. Its high fiber keeps hunger away for 4+ hours.
- **Choose Kamande if:** You have a **sensitive stomach prone to bloating**, or you need a **fast 20-minute meal prep** after work.

### Smart Meal Prep Tip:
Rotate both! Cook a bulk batch of Ndengu on Sunday for your weekday lunches, and keep Kamande on standby for fast 20-minute weeknight dinners.
